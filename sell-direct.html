    The FSBO playbook

    Selling it yourself in 5 steps

    Price it right

    Pull recent comparable sales within a half mile and 6 months; adjust for size and condition. Overpricing is the #1 reason FSBO homes sit.

    Prep & photograph

    Declutter, deep clean, fix the obvious, and invest in professional photos — listings with pro photos get more showings.

    List everywhere

    A flat-fee MLS listing syndicates to the big portals. Add a yard sign with a QR code.

    Show & negotiate

    Pre-qualify buyers, require proof of funds or a pre-approval, and compare net — not just price.

    Close cleanly

    A real estate attorney, title company or notary prepares documents and holds deposits in escrow.

    Selling FAQ

    Is a cash offer always lower than the market?

    Usually. Cash buyers price in repairs, holding costs and profit, so offers often land 5–20% below market value. In exchange you get speed, certainty and no showings. Always compare at least two cash offers against a market-value estimate.

    Can I really sell without an agent?

    Yes. For-sale-by-owner sales are legal everywhere in the U.S. and Canada. A flat-fee MLS listing (typically $300–$600) puts your home on the same databases agents search, and a real estate attorney or notary can handle the contract.

    Do I still have to pay the buyer's agent?

    Not necessarily. Since 2024, U.S. buyer-agent compensation is negotiable and no longer set through the MLS. Many sellers still offer a concession to attract agent-represented buyers — it's your call.

    How do I avoid lowball 'we buy houses' offers?

    Get multiple offers, ask for proof of funds, read the inspection-contingency terms, and be wary of buyers who drop the price after inspection. Never sign an exclusive agreement to receive an offer.

    What does selling cost besides commission?

    Expect roughly 1–3% for closing costs and transfer taxes, plus repairs, staging, moving and any concessions. The net proceeds calculator includes all of these.
