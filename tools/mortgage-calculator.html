    How this calculator works

    Principal and interest use the standard amortization formula: payment = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where L is the loan amount, r the monthly rate and n the number of payments.

    Canada mode converts the quoted annual rate to an effective monthly rate using semi-annual compounding, as required for Canadian fixed-rate mortgages: r = (1 + rate/2)1/6 − 1.

    PMI is added only when your down payment is under 20%. Conventional PMI commonly runs 0.3%–1.5% of the loan per year depending on credit and down payment; it can usually be removed once you reach 20% equity. Canadian insured mortgages use a one-time CMHC-style premium instead — add it to the loan amount if it applies to you.

    Pro tips

    • Each 1-point drop in rate on a $320,000 loan saves roughly $200–$220 a month.
    • Ask lenders for a Loan Estimate to compare APR, points and fees on the same day.
    • Shorter terms (15–20 years) cut total interest dramatically for a higher monthly payment.

    FAQ

    What is PITI?

    Principal, interest, taxes and insurance — the four core parts of a mortgage payment. Lenders use PITI to qualify you.

    How much should my mortgage payment be?

    A common guideline is to keep housing costs under 28%–31% of gross monthly income and total debts under 36%–43%.

    Why is my first payment mostly interest?

    Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is highest at the start. Each payment shifts more toward principal over time.
