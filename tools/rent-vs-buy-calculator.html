    Rent vs. buy calculator

    Buying builds equity, but it also brings closing costs, maintenance, property tax and selling costs. Renting keeps your down payment invested. This calculator compares both paths year by year and finds the break-even point.

    How this calculator works

    Buying net cost = down payment + closing costs + every month's mortgage, tax, insurance, maintenance and PMI − equity you'd keep after selling costs.

    Renting net cost = all rent paid − growth earned by investing the down payment, closing costs and any monthly difference when owning costs more than renting.

    The break-even year is the first year buying's net cost drops below renting's.

    Pro tips

    • The shorter your stay, the more renting tends to win because closing and selling costs dominate.
    • Be conservative with appreciation; national averages hide big local swings.
    • Include realistic maintenance — 1%–2% of value per year.

    FAQ

    Is renting throwing money away?

    No. Rent buys housing and flexibility; owning has its own unrecoverable costs (interest, taxes, maintenance, transaction fees).

    What's a good break-even horizon?

    Commonly 4–7 years in balanced markets; longer in expensive metros.

    Does this include tax benefits?

    No. Deductions only help if you itemize, which most households don't after the higher standard deduction.
