    Tree Removal Cost in 2026

    Removing a tree costs $300–$2,500 depending on height, diameter and risk. A medium 30–60 ft tree typically costs $600–$1,500; large or hazardous trees near homes can exceed $2,500. Stump grinding is usually extra at $100–$400 per stump.

    What drives the price

    Size

    Height and trunk diameter drive time and equipment.

    Location

    Trees near houses or lines must be lowered piece by piece.

    Condition

    Dead or rotten trees are less predictable and riskier.

    Access

    Backyard trees may need cranes or hand-carrying debris.

    Cleanup

    Wood hauling and stump grinding are often priced separately.

    Signs you need tree removal

    • Dead branches or bare canopy
    • Leaning toward structures
    • Cracks or cavities in the trunk
    • Mushrooms or fungus at the base
    • Roots damaging foundations or pipes

    DIY or hire a pro?

    Pro only

    Small trees under about 15 feet in open areas can be DIY. Anything near structures, power lines or requiring climbing should be handled by an insured arborist.

    How to save on tree removal

    • Hire an ISA-certified arborist for hazardous trees.
    • Ask if they will leave firewood to cut cost.
    • Check if your city requires a removal permit.
    • Combine multiple trees in one visit for a better rate.

    Questions to ask before you hire

    1. Are you insured for liability and workers' comp?
    2. Is stump grinding and debris removal included?
    3. Will you need a crane?
    4. Do you need a permit from the city?
    5. How will you protect my lawn and structures?

    Tree Removal FAQ

    Does insurance cover tree removal?

    Usually only when a tree falls on a covered structure.

    Do I need a permit?

    Many cities require permits for large or protected species.

    Can I remove a neighbor's tree?

    You can typically trim overhanging branches to the property line, but not remove a neighbor's tree.
