    Flooring Installation Cost in 2026

    New flooring typically costs $3.50–$11 per square foot installed. Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) is the value leader at $3.50–$7, engineered and solid hardwood run $8–$18, and tile ranges from $9–$20. For 500 square feet, most homeowners pay $2,000–$6,000.

    What drives the price

    Material

    Product cost varies 4–5× between LVP and premium hardwood.

    Subfloor prep

    Leveling, moisture barriers and repairs add $1–$5 per sq ft.

    Layout

    Herringbone and diagonal patterns add 15–30% waste and labor.

    Stairs

    Each stair tread costs $75–$200 to cover.

    Furniture moving

    Some installers charge to move furniture and appliances.

    Signs you need flooring installation

    • Squeaky, sagging or soft spots
    • Water-damaged or warped boards
    • Worn carpet and persistent odors
    • Cracked tiles or grout
    • Dated look before listing a home

    DIY or hire a pro?

    DIY-friendly

    Click-lock luxury vinyl plank and laminate are among the most DIY-friendly upgrades. Nail-down hardwood, tile and stairs are better left to installers.

    How to save on flooring installation

    • Buy 10% extra for waste (15% for patterns).
    • Let flooring acclimate 48–72 hours before installation.
    • Choose waterproof LVP for kitchens, baths and basements.
    • Refinish existing hardwood instead of replacing — often $3–$8 per sq ft.

    Questions to ask before you hire

    1. Is subfloor preparation included?
    2. What underlayment and moisture barrier will you use?
    3. How are transitions and baseboards handled?
    4. Is old flooring removal and disposal included?
    5. What is the installation warranty?

    Flooring Installation FAQ

    What's the most durable flooring?

    Porcelain tile and quality LVP resist water and scratches best.

    Can flooring go over existing floors?

    Floating floors can often go over flat, sound existing floors; carpet must be removed.

    Does hardwood add value?

    Yes — buyers consistently favor hardwood, and refinishing existing hardwood is a top pre-sale move.

    Resale value: Hardwood refinishing recovers 100%+; new hardwood ~70–80%.
