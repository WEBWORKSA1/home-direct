    Electrical Panel Upgrade Cost in 2026

    Upgrading from a 100-amp to a 200-amp panel typically costs $1,800–$4,500. If the utility service drop, meter base or underground feed must also be upgraded, total cost can reach $6,000 or more. EV chargers, heat pumps and kitchen remodels are the usual triggers.

    What drives the price

    Utility work

    Some utilities charge to upgrade the service drop or transformer.

    Panel location

    Moving the panel adds wiring and wall repair.

    Code corrections

    Grounding, bonding and AFCI/GFCI updates are often required at inspection.

    Permit fees

    Vary widely by city.

    Recall-era panels

    Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels should be replaced regardless of capacity.

    Signs you need electrical panel upgrade

    • Breakers tripping frequently
    • Flickering lights when appliances start
    • Fuses instead of breakers
    • Federal Pacific, Zinsco or Challenger brand panel
    • Planning an EV charger, heat pump or major remodel

    DIY or hire a pro?

    Pro only

    Panel and service work is lethal if done wrong and requires a permit and utility coordination. Always hire a licensed electrician.

    How to save on electrical panel upgrade

    • Do a load calculation first — a smart load manager can sometimes avoid a full upgrade.
    • Bundle EV charger and panel work in one permit.
    • Ask the utility about free service upgrades.
    • Schedule early; utility disconnect appointments book out.

    Questions to ask before you hire

    1. Is utility coordination and the permit included?
    2. Does the quote cover meter base and grounding upgrades?
    3. What brand panel and how many spaces?
    4. How long will power be off?
    5. Will you label every circuit?

    Electrical Panel Upgrade FAQ

    Do I need 200 amps?

    If you plan an EV charger plus electric heat or cooking, usually yes. A load calculation decides.

    How long does it take?

    Usually one day, with power off for 4–8 hours.

    Is a Federal Pacific panel dangerous?

    Many electricians and insurers consider them a fire risk due to breakers that may fail to trip.
