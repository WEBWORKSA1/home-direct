    Bathroom Remodel Cost in 2026

    Most bathroom remodels cost $8,000–$25,000. A small guest bath refresh can stay under $10,000, while a primary bath with a custom tile shower and double vanity often reaches $25,000–$35,000+. Tile labor and waterproofing are the biggest variables.

    What drives the price

    Tile scope

    Floor-to-ceiling tile and niches add labor.

    Plumbing moves

    Relocating toilets and drains is expensive, especially on slabs.

    Fixtures

    Thermostatic valves and frameless glass add cost.

    Ventilation

    Code requires adequate exhaust fans.

    Accessibility

    Curbless showers and grab-bar blocking need planning.

    Signs you need bathroom remodel

    • Soft floors around the toilet or tub
    • Cracked or missing grout and caulk
    • Mold that returns quickly
    • Outdated fixtures and poor lighting
    • Aging-in-place needs

    DIY or hire a pro?

    Skilled DIY

    Vanity swaps, toilets, paint and lighting fixtures are DIY-friendly. Shower waterproofing is where DIY fails most often — a leaking pan can rot the subfloor for years before you notice.

    How to save on bathroom remodel

    • Keep the toilet and shower drain where they are.
    • Use large-format tile to reduce grout lines and labor.
    • Spend on waterproofing (e.g., membrane systems), save on decorative tile.
    • Buy fixtures yourself only if the contractor agrees to install and warranty them.

    Questions to ask before you hire

    1. Which shower waterproofing system will you use?
    2. Is subfloor repair priced separately?
    3. Are fixtures and tile allowances included?
    4. How will ventilation be vented outside?
    5. What is the timeline from demo to final?

    Bathroom Remodel FAQ

    How long does a bathroom remodel take?

    Two to five weeks of construction for most bathrooms.

    What's the best ROI bathroom upgrade?

    Midrange updates — new vanity, tile, fixtures and lighting — typically recover 60–75% at resale.

    Can a tub be removed if it's the only one?

    Keeping at least one tub in the home is often recommended for families with young children.
